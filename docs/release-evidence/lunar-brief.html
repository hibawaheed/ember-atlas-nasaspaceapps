Limitation

Saffire tests were microgravity fires with forced airflow inside an uncrewed Cygnus spacecraft. No measured lunar-gravity fire test is in this atlas, and NASA's PSI archive holds no partial-gravity combustion data (per the challenge statement). The lab's flame picture is an authored illustration. An artistic response model, not CFD, a temperature estimate, an extinction boundary, or a fire-risk score. Model coefficients are authored for the demo, not fitted to NASA measurements.

Supporting findings

  • Flame spread varied as airflow and oxygen changed (BASS-II; Oxygen changed during each run). In the published BASS-II PMMA runs, flame-spread rate changed between successive airflow settings while oxygen also fell. In run M3, airflow rose from 10 to 20 cm/s while spread fell from 0.029 to 0.024 mm/s; in run M7, airflow and spread fell together (10 → 3 cm/s, 0.106 → 0.070 mm/s). Burning and Suppression of Solids–II (BASS-II) Summary Report
  • An enriched atmosphere changes the flame (Saffire-V; Qualitative · specific Saffire-V conditions). Saffire-V lowered spacecraft pressure and increased oxygen concentration. NASA reported more energetic flames at elevated oxygen levels and faster burning on thinner PMMA ribs. Saffire Ignites New Discoveries in Space
  • Large fires measured near exploration-cabin oxygen levels (Saffire VI; Microgravity · one flow speed). Saffire VI burned SIBAL cloth at 31.0% oxygen and 54.1 kPa (VI-2), two-sided acrylic (PMMA) at 30.3% and 54.6 kPa (VI-3), and one-sided PMMA at 28.8% and 55.2 kPa (VI-4), all in 20 cm/s concurrent flow in microgravity (Table 1). Peak heat release was 3217 W, 3080 W and 1507 W (Table 2). Preliminary Results from the Saffire VI Experiment (ICES-2024-365)
